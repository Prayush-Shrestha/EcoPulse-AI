8. KEY OBSERVATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The rolling z-score flagged 45,325 minutes (2.218%), while the Isolation Forest flagged 8,809 (0.431%).
- The methods agree on 6,291 minutes (Jaccard 13.15%), which is expected because they model different notions of deviation.
- The z-score is univariate on active power; the Isolation Forest also uses voltage, reactive power, intensity, sub-metering and calendar context.
- Detection percentages differ across splits; this reflects changing household behaviour over four years as well as the train-only threshold.

9. LIMITATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- No reliable ground-truth anomaly labels exist. Accuracy, precision,
  recall and F1 are therefore not reported and would be fabricated.
- Detected observations are deviation candidates, not confirmed faults.
  A high reading can be normal behaviour; a fault can produce a normal
  reading.
- The Isolation Forest contamination setting is a prior assumption, not an
  estimated fault prevalence.
- The rolling z-score assumes approximate local stability and is sensitive
  to autocorrelation and heavy tails; the fixed 3.0 threshold is a
  convention, not a calibrated probability.
- Interpolated minutes were excluded, and long missing intervals are absent
  from the source, so gap-adjacent behaviour is under-represented.
- No weather, occupancy, appliance identity or tariff context is available.
- Anomaly scores measure statistical deviation from learned patterns, not physical causation or fault severity.
